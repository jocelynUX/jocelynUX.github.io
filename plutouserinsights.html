Pluto VR · Exploratory Data Analysis

Regional Growth Insights for Pluto VR

I looked at six months of anonymized PlutoSphere session data to better understand where people were using the product and whether those patterns could help with infrastructure planning.

I cleaned and explored the data in Tableau Prep and Tableau, looking at usage by country and how it changed from February through July 2023. When infrastructure constraints ruled out the original cost-saving approach, I shifted the question: could the same data help us think more clearly about where future international or localization research might start?

01

Starting With a Broad Question

This project started with a fairly open-ended request to look at our user data and see what I could learn from it.

One question I was interested in was whether geography could tell us something useful about infrastructure. If enough usage was concentrated in particular regions, could that eventually help us make more targeted decisions about where resources were needed?

I started by looking at sessions by country and how those numbers changed over time. I was not trying to prove a specific hypothesis. I was mostly trying to understand what was there and whether any patterns were strong enough to be useful.

Initial business question

Could the geographic distribution of PlutoSphere sessions help inform infrastructure decisions and potentially reduce costs?

02

Preparing the Data

I used Tableau Prep to clean and organize the session data before bringing it into Tableau.

I structured the data so I could compare countries, track changes over time, separate U.S. from non-U.S. activity, and explore countries with higher or similar usage patterns.

This was exploratory analysis, not forecasting. I was looking at what had already happened and trying to understand which patterns were worth paying attention to.

03

What I Found

I wanted the main findings to be understandable before asking anyone to explore the dashboards themselves.

01

PlutoSphere was primarily used in the United States, but international activity was visible

What I saw

The U.S. accounted for the largest share of sessions. Once I separated the non-U.S. activity, differences between international markets became much easier to see.

What it suggested

There was enough non-U.S. activity to make country-level comparisons useful.

02

Canada and Germany stood out among non-U.S. countries

What I saw

Canada and Germany recorded substantially more sessions than other international countries in the dataset. I also used Tableau’s clustering feature to compare usage patterns over time.

What it suggested

These markets were reasonable places to investigate more closely if Pluto VR later pursued international expansion. The data did not tell me why usage was higher or whether localization would increase it.

03

Geographic usage could inform more than one business question

What I saw

The session data showed clear geographic differences in where PlutoSphere was being used.

What it suggested

Even after the original infrastructure path closed, those patterns could still help identify which international markets were worth learning more about.

04

The Useful Question Changed

My original interest was whether the geographic patterns could support an infrastructure decision. That path eventually closed because our infrastructure provider couldn't support the provisioning change we were considering.

The data had not become less useful. It just could not support the action I had originally been exploring.

So I looked again at what it could reasonably tell us. Pluto VR had users outside the U.S., and some markets were much more active than others. If the company later had the resources to invest in international growth, those existing patterns could help narrow where we might want to learn more first.

Started with

Can geography help inform infrastructure decisions?

→

Reframed to

Which international markets are already showing enough activity to deserve closer attention?

05

Exploring the Patterns

Interactive Tableau dashboard

Geographic Distribution of PlutoSphere Sessions

This dashboard shows anonymized PlutoSphere sessions by country from February through July 2023. I added filters for month and for U.S. versus non-U.S. activity because the much larger U.S. numbers made the international patterns harder to see.

What to look for: Filtering to non-U.S. sessions makes the differences between countries much clearer. The map shows where activity was happening, not why those differences existed.

Interactive Tableau dashboard

What to look for: The clusters helped me organize patterns already present in the data. I treated them as an exploratory aid, not as a ranking of which countries Pluto VR should enter next.

Presenting the Findings

I presented the analysis to Pluto VR’s two founders and the engineering team.

I did not want the presentation to become a tour of every Tableau view I had made. I focused instead on the parts that seemed useful for product and technical decisions.

One thing I tried to keep clear was the difference between a pattern and a recommendation. Canada and Germany having more sessions was something I could see in the data. Suggesting that we look more closely at those markets was my interpretation of what that pattern could mean.

07

What the Project Produced

  • Analysis: Cleaned and organized six months of anonymized session data into views that made geographic patterns easier to compare.
  • Findings: Identified Canada and Germany as notable among non-U.S. markets while keeping the limits of that observation explicit.
  • Communication: Built interactive Tableau views and presented the findings to company leadership and engineering.
  • Decision support: Reframed the work after the original infrastructure question became infeasible, creating a more focused starting point for future international research.

There was no immediate localization decision that came out of this work. The useful result was that we knew more than we had before: where people were already using PlutoSphere, which international markets looked different from the rest, and where future questions could start.

08

What the Data Could Not Tell Me

PlutoSphere was still an early-stage product, so I would not treat six months of session data as evidence of a mature market trend.

The analysis also could not explain why one country had more usage than another. I was not measuring language preference, market awareness, population differences, or what had originally brought individual users to the product.

The data could help me ask

Where are people using the product, and what patterns stand out?

It could not answer on its own

Why is that happening, and what should we do about it?

09

What I Learned

Sometimes the question changes

I started with one possible use for the data and ended somewhere else. That meant looking again at what the evidence could actually support once one option was off the table.

A pattern is not an explanation

Canada and Germany stood out clearly. The harder question was what, if anything, we should infer from that. That made me more careful about separating observation from interpretation.

The analysis has to connect to a real decision

It is easy to keep digging because a dataset is interesting. Presenting this work pushed me to focus on what was actually useful to the people making decisions.

Exploring data without forcing the conclusion

The most useful outcome was not the answer I expected to find.

This project gave me practice working with an ambiguous question, organizing imperfect early-product data, adapting when the original direction changed, and explaining both the findings and their limits to people making product and technical decisions.
